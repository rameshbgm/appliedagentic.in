# Introduction: Why Product Development Is Changing

Every organization — whether it makes running shoes, electric vehicles, skincare products, or software — goes through some version of a product development process. Ideas get generated, concepts get designed, prototypes get built, products get tested, launched, and improved.

Historically, this process was linear and slow. It moved at the pace of human creativity and human hands. AI is changing that fundamentally — and agentic AI is taking that change several steps further.

In this section, we explore how both **generative AI** (which creates content and designs) and **agentic AI** (which operates independently over time to pursue goals) are reshaping how products come to life.

## The Structure of the Product Development Lifecycle (PDLC)

While every organization labels the phases slightly differently, most product development journeys follow the same core structure:

| Phase | What Happens |
|-------|-------------|
| **Ideation and Requirements** | Concept generation, stakeholder interviews, competitive research |
| **Design** | UX/UI design, interaction flows, early system architecture |
| **Prototyping** | Building low- or high-fidelity versions for internal or external feedback |
| **Development** | Writing code, integrating systems, preparing for launch |
| **Testing and QA** | Validating functionality, reliability, and security |
| **Launch** | Shipping the product to users |
| **Post-Launch Iteration** | Monitoring usage, gathering feedback, making improvements |

> 💡 **What This Means**
> Whether you're developing a virtual reality headset, a toothbrush holder, a mobile app, or a financial product, these phases apply universally. Understanding *where* AI can intervene at each stage is the key to unlocking its potential across your specific organization.

## Generative AI in the PDLC: Producing at Scale

**Generative AI** is already being used throughout the product development cycle to produce content, designs, structures, and data models based on patterns it has learned. It *augments* human creativity and speeds up tasks that once required hours of manual work.

Here are four compelling real-world examples:


### Nike: AI-Designed Footwear

Nike launched the **A.I.R. initiative** — a generative AI-driven collaboration between designers and elite athletes. Using proprietary AI models trained on performance data, biomechanics, and individual athlete preferences, Nike's team rapidly generates and iterates on footwear designs.

The process includes:
- Creating visual concepts
- Refining them through 3D modeling
- Producing physical prototypes via 3D printing

By anchoring each design in individualized athletic needs, the process enhances both creativity and functional performance *(Digital Defynd, 2024)*.


### Tesla: AI-Driven Vehicle Component Design

Tesla has adopted generative AI in its vehicle design workflows, using AI-driven tools to explore thousands of design variations for vehicle components. Engineers input performance constraints — aerodynamics, material weight, structural rigidity — and AI rapidly generates optimized forms that human designers might never have considered *(NexGen Cloud, 2025)*.

> 🌍 **Real-World Example**
> Imagine having 10,000 engineers working simultaneously on every possible version of a car door hinge — each one optimized for a different combination of weight, strength, and manufacturability. Generative AI does exactly that, in hours rather than years.


### L'Oréal: Sustainable Formulations at Scale

L'Oréal has deployed generative AI not just to formulate personalized skincare and cosmetics, but also to optimize sustainable raw material sourcing and content production.

In partnership with IBM, L'Oréal developed a proprietary generative model aimed at:
- Accelerating formulation design
- Reducing energy consumption and material waste
- Supporting sustainability goals *(McMillan, 2025)*

These initiatives go beyond analysis — the AI *produces* both formulations and creative content, signaling a genuine shift toward creative agency at scale.


### Crocs: User-Driven Personalization

Crocs partnered with AI fashion design platform **ABLO** to empower customers to co-create personalized Jibbitz shoe charms using generative AI.

Users submit photos or text prompts, and ABLO's AI system generates custom charm designs matched to various artistic styles. Once a design is chosen, it's produced and sold through Crocs' platform *(Fast Company, 2025)*.

This user-driven generative process enhances Crocs' core personalization strategy and accelerates the design-to-production pipeline — demonstrating that generative AI in product development can be *customer-facing*, not just internal.


> 💡 **What This Means**
> In each of these generative AI cases, the AI serves as an intelligent *assistant*: producing options, suggesting directions, and speeding up ideation — but still ultimately under human direction. Generative AI amplifies what humans can create. Agentic AI takes this further.

# Agentic AI in the PDLC: Autonomous Action Over Time

**Agentic AI** takes generative capabilities and adds something more powerful: the capacity to operate *across time*, act *independently*, pursue *goals*, and manage *sequences of tasks*. Rather than being reactive (waiting for a prompt), these systems are proactive and adaptive.

Here are four examples of agentic AI in product development:


### Oracle: Autonomous Sales Content Creation

Oracle developed agentic AI systems that support sales professionals by *autonomously* performing multi-step writing and communication tasks.

These AI agents:
- Scan customer data across platforms
- Analyze buyer behavior
- Draft personalized outreach messages
- Refine tone based on context
- Adapt content for different communication channels

All without human prompting at each step. The system doesn't just write — it *decides* what to write, why, and when to send it. In this context, agentic AI plays the role of co-creator, helping scale personalized communication as a core business capability *(Nellis, 2025)*.


### General Motors: Engineering Through Autonomous Iteration

GM has integrated agentic AI into its engineering processes by adopting AI-powered generative design platforms — most notably in collaboration with Autodesk.

Engineers define performance goals (improved strength-to-weight ratio, manufacturability constraints), and the AI:
1. Iterates autonomously through numerous design variants
2. Evaluates each version against engineering metrics
3. Selects optimal solutions — such as lightweight, 3D-printable vehicle brackets
4. Moves chosen designs directly to prototyping

This loop — *set goals → generate options → evaluate → iterate* — embodies real-world agentic AI producing tangible engineering improvements *(DeNittis, 2023)*.


### Hyperganic: AI-Designed Rocket Engines

Hyperganic's approach to rocket engine design exemplifies agentic AI in engineering. Their system operates across an iterative design loop, taking performance goals (thrust, cooling efficiency, material constraints) and autonomously:
- Generating solutions
- Refining internal structures (like cooling channels) based on thermodynamic performance
- Producing build-ready models for 3D printing

The result: a **hot-fire-tested aerospike rocket engine** that went from specification to tested prototype in weeks — a process that would previously have taken years *(PC Gamer, 2025)*.

> ⚠️ **Why This Matters**
> This isn't just faster design. The AI is *self-directing design evolution*. Engineers shift from hands-on execution to high-level specification and judgment — a fundamental change in what human expertise is needed for.


### IKEA: AI as a Creative Collaborator

In a collaboration with its independent design lab SPACE10, IKEA employed agentic AI to explore new directions in furniture design.

The AI, trained on decades of IKEA's archival catalogs, functioned as a **creative collaborator**, autonomously producing and refining hundreds of design concepts across aesthetic themes, time periods, and functional constraints.

Designers could interact with these outputs — guiding the AI to explore new branches of style, form, or era. This iterative, responsive process positioned AI not as a tool, but as a *partner* in navigating IKEA's design heritage and future possibilities *(Fast Company, 2023)*.

## How AI Transforms Each Stage of the PDLC

Pulling back from individual examples, here's how AI creates value across every stage of the product development lifecycle:

| PDLC Stage | How Agentic AI Contributes |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Ideation** | Identifies whitespace in the market by analyzing trends, behaviors, and user feedback across data sources |
| **Prototyping** | Generates, tests, and revises digital prototypes in loops, often using user simulation data |
| **Development** | Multiple AI agents write code, review each other's work, and manage version control autonomously |
| **Testing & QA** | Continuously builds and executes test suites; monitors changes and adapts tests in real time |
| **Launch** | Coordinates documentation, deployment pipelines, and compliance checks |
| **Post-Launch** | Observes usage patterns, proposes iterations, or triggers redesigns autonomously |

## Organizational Impact: Why This Changes Everything

The strategic advantages of agentic AI in product development go beyond any single example:

- **Speed**: Product development cycles shrink dramatically, allowing for rapid iteration. What once took months can now take weeks — or days
- **Cost-Efficiency**: Labor-intensive tasks like QA, documentation, and design review can be handled by agents, reducing the human hours required
- **Scalability**: Organizations can manage multiple product lines or run parallel experiments simultaneously, without proportionally scaling headcount
- **Quality and Consistency**: With standardized logic and continual monitoring, agentic systems enforce best practices without burnout or distraction

> 💡 **What This Means**
> These effects also *reshape teams*. As AI absorbs execution-heavy roles, human contributors increasingly shift toward judgment, strategy, and values-driven decision-making. The question is no longer "can a human do this?" but "should a human be doing this, or should AI?"

# Getting Ready: What Organizations Need to Shift

The transition to agentic AI in product development doesn't require reinventing everything, but it does require intentional changes in four areas:

### People
Train designers, developers, and managers to *work with* AI agents — including how to prompt them effectively, supervise their outputs, and know when to trust them versus when to question them.

### Tools
Invest in platforms that allow for **agent orchestration** — frameworks that connect large language models to APIs, data sources, and task management systems. The tools matter, but they're only as good as the people using them.

### Processes
Modify agile workflows to incorporate AI contributions. Think about AI as a team member in daily stand-ups, sprint planning, and release reviews. It should be a participant, not just a background tool.

### Mindset
*This is the hardest one.* Move from **command-and-control** leadership toward **collaborative supervision**. Think of AI not as replacing human teammates, but as augmenting what teams can take on. The goal is more of the *right* work, with better results.

> 🌍 **Real-World Example**
> A consumer goods company that introduces agentic AI into its packaging design process doesn't eliminate the design team. Instead, designers go from spending 60% of their time creating initial mockups to spending 80% of their time providing creative direction, evaluating AI-generated options, and making strategic brand decisions. Their work becomes more interesting and more impactful.

# Key Takeaways

- **Generative AI** augments human creativity and speeds up design and content production — Nike, Tesla, L'Oréal, and Crocs are already using it at scale
- **Agentic AI** goes further: it operates autonomously over time, pursuing goals and managing multi-step tasks — Oracle, GM, Hyperganic, and IKEA demonstrate this in engineering and creative contexts
- AI creates value at *every stage* of the product development lifecycle — from ideation to post-launch iteration
- The organizational impact is real: faster cycles, lower costs, greater scalability, and more consistent quality
- Success requires shifts in **people** (training), **tools** (orchestration platforms), **processes** (agile adaptation), and most importantly **mindset** (from control to collaboration)
