# What the Capstone Is

The capstone is your final assignment for this course. It is not a test of memory — it is a test of *application*.

You will produce a **2–5 page document** that outlines how AI could be integrated into a specific function or process within your organisation (or an organisation you know well).

> 💡 **Important**: You may not have access to all the information needed to answer every section. That is expected and acceptable. Where information is missing, specify *how* it could be obtained — through internal resources, external expertise, or additional data collection. Showing you know what you *don't* know is itself a mark of sophisticated thinking.

## The Eight Elements of Your Capstone Document

Your proposal must address all eight of the following elements:

### 1. Business Context
*What is the problem you are solving?*

Describe:
- The organisation and the specific function or process you're addressing
- The business challenge, inefficiency, or opportunity AI could address
- The current state: how is this done today? What are the limitations?
- Why now? What makes this the right moment to act?

**Good business context** is specific, not generic. "Improve customer service" is too vague. "Reduce first-contact resolution time for billing queries from 8 minutes to under 3 minutes" is a business context.


### 2. Technology Choice
*What AI solution will you use, and why?*

Describe:
- The type of AI agent or system you are proposing (e.g., voice AI, document processing agent, decision support agent)
- The specific platform or model you would use (e.g., a vendor solution, open-source model, or custom build)
- Why this technology is the right fit for the problem
- What alternatives you considered and why you ruled them out

**Useful reference**: The agent toolkit from Module 5 and the platform comparison from Module 2 will help you structure this section.


### 3. Cost Considerations
*What will this cost, and what will it return?*

Address:
- Estimated implementation costs (development, integration, licensing, infrastructure)
- Ongoing operational costs (monitoring, maintenance, model updates)
- Expected ROI: what savings, revenue gains, or efficiency improvements do you anticipate?
- Time to break-even: how long before the investment pays off?

**Useful reference**: Module 1's activity on AI system costs and pricing helps you think through realistic financial estimates.

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: Underestimating integration costs. As Module 6 warned, connecting AI to legacy systems often costs 2–3× more than the AI development itself.


### 4. Security Plan
*How will you protect the system and the data it uses?*

Cover:
- What data does the AI handle? What are the sensitivity and privacy implications?
- What are the specific security risks (prompt injection, data leakage, adversarial attacks)?
- What controls will you implement (access controls, encryption, audit logging, guardrails)?
- What happens if the AI is compromised or produces harmful output?

**Useful reference**: Module 4's cybersecurity frameworks and the Cyber Kill Chain provide structure for identifying and mitigating threats.


### 5. Change Management and Training
*How will you bring your people along?*

Address:
- Who is affected by this AI deployment (employees, customers, managers)?
- What are the likely sources of resistance or concern?
- How will you communicate the change — and when?
- What training will be provided, and in what format?
- How will you measure whether adoption is succeeding?

**Useful reference**: Module 6's ADKAR model and Kotter's 8-step framework provide proven structures for managing this.

> 💡 **Don't skip this**: Research shows that 70% of change programmes fail not because the technology doesn't work — but because people don't adopt it. Change management is not optional.


### 6. Scaling Strategy
*How will you grow this beyond the initial deployment?*

Describe:
- What does a successful pilot look like? How will you know when you're ready to scale?
- What additional functions, teams, or use cases could this AI serve as it matures?
- What technical infrastructure will you need as volume grows?
- What governance processes will you put in place to manage scaling responsibly?

**Useful reference**: Module 5's AI maturity cycle (crawl–walk–run) provides a framework for thinking about stages of adoption.


### 7. Governance and Compliance
*How will you ensure the AI operates within legal and ethical boundaries?*

Cover:
- What regulations apply to your use case (GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, sector-specific rules)?
- How will you document the AI system for compliance purposes?
- Who is responsible for AI governance in your organisation?
- What oversight mechanisms will you put in place (human review, audit trails, model cards)?
- How will you handle compliance updates as regulations evolve?

**Useful reference**: Module 7's regulatory framework overview and documentation readiness content is directly applicable here.


### 8. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
*How will you know if the AI is working?*

Define:
- Operational KPIs (task completion rate, processing time, error rate)
- Financial KPIs (cost per interaction, cost savings realised)
- User experience KPIs (satisfaction scores, abandonment rate)
- Governance KPIs (hallucination rate, compliance violations)
- Learning KPIs (is performance improving over time?)

**Useful reference**: Module 6's KPI framework by agent type gives you templates you can adapt directly.

> 💡 **Key principle**: KPIs must connect to business outcomes, not just technical metrics from the vendor dashboard. "Token usage" is not a KPI. "Customer support ticket resolution rate" is.

## The Ethical Dimension of Your Capstone

Before you finalise your capstone document, run it through the ethical lens from this module:

**Stakeholder Impact Check**:
- [ ] Who are the employees affected? How are you supporting them through the transition?
- [ ] Who are the customers affected? How are their interests protected?
- [ ] Are there any groups who might be disproportionately disadvantaged by this AI system?
- [ ] Who is accountable if the AI produces a harmful outcome?

**Transparency Check**:
- [ ] Can you explain how the AI makes decisions in plain language?
- [ ] Is there a mechanism for humans to review and override AI decisions?
- [ ] Would you be comfortable explaining this proposal on the front page of a newspaper?

**Fairness Check**:
- [ ] What training data will the AI use? Does it reflect bias from historical practices?
- [ ] How will you test for and monitor bias after deployment?

# Tips for a Strong Capstone

1. **Be specific** — Generic proposals score poorly. Name the function, the problem, the technology, and the metrics.

2. **Acknowledge uncertainty honestly** — You won't have all the answers. Say what you don't know and how you'd find out.

3. **Show the connections** — Reference specific concepts, frameworks, and examples from the course. This demonstrates integrated understanding, not just surface recall.

4. **Don't neglect the human elements** — Change management and ethics sections are often under-developed. They are equally important as the technical sections.

5. **Think about failure modes** — What could go wrong? How have you designed to catch it? Anticipating failure is a sign of mature thinking.
